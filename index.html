01 / THE OPERATIONAL PROBLEM

When the key cannot be found, work stops.

On complex data-centre projects, multiple APs may manage overlapping isolations, documents and working parties. If keys, lockout boxes or document recipients cannot be located promptly, planned switching and energisation can be delayed.

01

Missing key information

Time is spent identifying which key belongs to an isolation point and who currently holds it.

02

Unclear document ownership

Issued documents may be difficult to trace, particularly when the recipient is off-site or cannot be contacted.

03

Delayed next activity

Investigations and reconciliations consume AP time and can affect the commissioning programme.

The design principle

Give every lock, key, box, document and authorised person a defined identity and an accountable place in one controlled process.

03 / THE END-TO-END PROCESS

Follow one document from preparation to close-out.

Select each stage for a plain-English explanation of what happens physically, what is recorded, and what must be checked.

Non-negotiable safety boundary

A displayed lock, drawing symbol or document status is an administrative record—not proof that apparatus is isolated, proved dead or earthed. The AP/SAP must apply the governing electrical safety rules and verify the actual plant condition. Missing or inconsistent key information requires investigation and escalation; it is not permission to proceed.

05 / CONTROL, LIMITATIONS & ASSURANCE

Designed for accountability. Not yet approved for live operations.

Physical accountability

Every key and box has an assigned location. A missing key hook carries a physical movement tag, while the digital register is intended to hold the detailed custody history.

Access to the fourth cabinet is restricted to nominated APs/SAPs using a signed-out common cabinet key, subject to the approved site procedure.

Digital accountability

Intended controls include user identity, access permissions, document lifecycle, timestamped events, alerts, backups and audit evidence.

Client data, site drawings and personnel records require formal security assessment, permission and acceptance before production use.

Demonstration status — important

The reviewed V11 package is a demonstration. Its README states that new documents and cancellations are stored in each device's browser; a shared database is required for a reliable site-wide register. Earlier device-to-device testing does not, by itself, establish that this particular packaged version provides synchronised production data.

Do not use demonstration statuses, example records or drawings to authorise work, issue safety documents or establish the safety of apparatus.

Before operational deployment

  • Agree site-specific electrical safety rules, authorisation limits and key-custody responsibilities.
  • Obtain written permission from the asset owner for engineering drawings and associated information.
  • Verify apparatus, lock numbers, cabinet locations, box IDs and all drawing revisions on site.
  • Implement a secured shared backend, role controls, audit trails and reliable multi-user synchronisation.
  • Test collisions, missing keys, lost connectivity, expired documents, cancellation and reconciliation.
  • Complete cybersecurity assessment, independent testing, training and formal client acceptance.

06 / CLIENT OVERVIEW

Less searching. More predictable control.

The proposal addresses a practical source of avoidable delay: uncertainty over the whereabouts of isolation keys, lockout boxes and active safety documents.

Faster retrieval

A designated location and a searchable custody record are intended to reduce time spent tracing keys and boxes.

Clearer ownership

AP/CP registers and document links make it easier to identify the person responsible for an active document.

More reliable planning

Visible document durations and key allocations support earlier resolution of conflicts before switching or commissioning.

IMPLEMENTATION MODEL

Start before the first energisation.

Design the lock numbering, cabinets, drawings and operating rules at mobilisation; train the team; then introduce the approved digital controls. Later joiners learn one consistent process rather than reconstructing local practice.
